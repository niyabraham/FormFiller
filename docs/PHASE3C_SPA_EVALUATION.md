# Phase 3C — SPA browser reliability

Branch `phase3c-spa-browser-reliability`, from `d3a132de393e51a457234757e686c16c190af94d` (Phase 3B-B). Committed as `1d1dbd0` on that branch.
Retrieval, scoring, thresholds, `plan.py`, `retrieve.py` and `validate.py` are untouched. No LLMs, embeddings, RAG, or live third-party sites.

## What the browser layer supported before, and what was missing (at `d3a132d`)
Supported: one page and its same-process iframes; native input/select/textarea/radio/checkbox; re-discovery after fills (3 rounds) for controls that appear synchronously; read-back verification; optional submit.
Missing (each is now a fixture): any navigation between wizard steps; waiting for async rendering; unmounted-then-mounted steps (control ids restarted at c1 and collided); open shadow DOM; ARIA comboboxes (typed text, never committed); disabled controls (a fill waited out the 5 s timeout); detection of dialogs, popups, reloads, or values reset by navigation; any record of *why* a control was not found.

## Design (minimal, policy-gated)
* **Default behaviour is unchanged**: `run_form(..., policy=None)` runs the old single-page path. Navigation happens only with a `DiscoveryPolicy` (`formfill/web/steps.py`).
* **Discovery layer** (`discover.py`): walks open shadow roots; discovers `role=combobox` as kind `combobox`; control ids are unique for the life of the page (`window.__ffCounter`); reports `opaque_custom_element` notes (closed/empty shadow hosts) instead of silently missing them.
* **Execution layer** (`execute.py`): combobox fill = open, pick the option whose text equals the answer, require the popup to close; read-back for div comboboxes. Playwright `fill` already fires real input events, so framework-controlled inputs needed no change (verified by the `controlled` fixture).
* **Navigation policy**: press only a button whose text matches an allow list (next/continue/proceed/save and continue/…) and not a deny list (submit, finish, confirm, pay, sign, certify, back, …) **and whose HTML metadata is safe**. Per step: settle (no DOM mutations, no pending fetch/XHR, no `aria-busy`), discover, fill, re-discover until nothing new, verify while the step is on screen, press Next, settle, re-verify earlier answers (reset detection).
* **Submit-type refusal (review round 2, extended in round 3).** Button discovery covers `button`, `[role=button]`, `input[type=button|submit|image]` and records tag, effective `type`, form ownership (including the `form=` attribute), `formAction` and, for links, the `href`. A control is *submit-type* when it is a `<button>` or `<input>` whose effective type is `submit` or `image` (an image input's label is its `alt`) **and** it belongs to a form (so a type-less `<button>Next</button>` inside a form is refused, because HTML makes it a submit button; the same markup outside any form is allowed, because it cannot submit anything). Links that leave the page are refused. The decision is made from the metadata *before* any click; the post-click submit-event counter remains only as a second line of defence. `DiscoveryPolicy.allow_submit_type_navigation` (default `False`) is the explicit opt-in for SPAs that intercept a submit button; it never unlocks commit-style labels or off-page links. Stop reason: `submit_type_next_refused`.
* **Settling is enforced (review round 2).** The `settle()` result is checked at step start, after every fill round and after pressing Next. If the page does not go quiet within `settle_timeout_ms`, or the document changed underneath, the run stops with `page_not_settled` / `document_navigation` and nothing further is filled or clicked. A run stopped this way can no longer report `FILLED` (it reports `NEEDS_REVIEW` with a warning) and `submit=True` is skipped.
* **Uncertain completion is incomplete (review round 3).** `no_safe_next_control` is ambiguous: it is what the genuine last step looks like and also what an unrecognised Next ("Onward", an icon, another language) looks like, and the page does not say which. It is therefore an *incomplete* stop: `navigation.completion = "unconfirmed"` with a `completion_unconfirmed` note, status `NEEDS_REVIEW` (never `FILLED`), a warning, and `submit=True` is skipped with the reason recorded. Consequently a policy walk is never submitted automatically, even when it did reach the real last step. Submitting after a walk needs a separate, explicit final-step policy, to be designed later; absence of a Next button is not treated as evidence of the last step. The no-policy single-page path is unchanged.
* **Safe stops** recorded in `result.navigation.stop_reason`: `no_safe_next_control`, `submit_type_next_refused`, `page_not_settled`, `next_disabled`, `dialog_on_navigation` (dismissed, never accepted), `popup_opened` (closed), `document_navigation`, `submit_event_observed`, `no_change_after_next`, `max_steps`. Attestation checkboxes stay REVIEW through the unchanged plan rule.
* **Submission safety, stated precisely.** *Default policy:* submit-type navigation controls (form-owned `<button>`/`<input>` submit, image input, off-page link) are refused before any click and no commit-labelled button is pressed, so the walk presses no control that HTML marks as a form submitter; and because every walk ends with a stop reason in `INCOMPLETE_STOPS` (completion is never confirmed), `run_form(submit=True)` skips the submit routine after a walk until a separate final-step policy is designed. *Explicit opt-in* (`allow_submit_type_navigation=True`): the walk may click a submit-type control labelled like a Next button. This is risky: if the portal does not intercept the click, it is a real submission. The post-click submit-event counter (`submit_event_observed`) is only a second line of defence: it runs after the click, sees DOM `submit` events in the main document only, and cannot detect script-driven submissions (`fetch`/XHR posts, `form.submit()`, submissions from an iframe). This report does not claim that every possible submission is prevented; the conservative default is unchanged.
* Disabled controls are deferred, not filled; if still disabled after the step, a `control_stayed_disabled` note is recorded.
* The walk records `first_seen` / `first_ready` (step, round) for every control, which the evaluator compares with the ground-truth stage.

## Fixtures and ground truth
`benchmarks/spa/` — 26 local pages generated by `build_fixtures.py` from one declaration (also writes `ground_truth.json`, `source.json`), served by a localhost server with an artificial-latency endpoint. Ground truth lists which controls are discoverable at each stage, expected answers, unsupported controls, planted resets and expected stop reasons. The app's own state (`window.__state`) is the truth for "was it committed", independent of the DOM the pipeline reads. Fixture labels were chosen so retrieval answers; retrieval was not tuned.

## Limitations and remaining safety concerns
* **Navigation-control discovery is narrower than field discovery.** Fields are found in the main document, open shadow roots (recursively) and same-process iframes. The Next/Continue scan (`_BUTTONS_JS` via `page.evaluate`, and the submit-event counter) looks at the main document only: it does not recurse into open shadow roots or iframes. A portal whose navigation controls live inside an open shadow root or an iframe will fill the fields it can see, then stop safely as `no_safe_next_control` (`NEEDS_REVIEW`, completion unconfirmed) even though its fields were discoverable. No fixture covers this case; it is a known gap, not a measured one. Closing it would need its own review because it widens where the policy may click.
* Closed shadow roots and cross-origin iframes are not readable (reported, not filled).
* Allow/deny label lists are English heuristics; an unusual Next label is a safe stop, not a guess.
* The last step is indistinguishable from a trap labelled "Submit" or from an unrecognised Next: all stop with `no_safe_next_control`. **Consequence (deliberate): a policy walk is never auto-submitted and never reports `FILLED`, even when it reached the real last step.** Submission after a walk needs a separate explicit final-step policy.
* **New:** the submit-type rule is conservative. A real SPA whose Next is a form submit button intercepted by JavaScript is refused by default and needs the explicit opt-in; the opt-in cannot tell an intercepting handler from a real submission until after the click. Markup cannot reveal a `<div role=button>` whose script submits a form or posts with `fetch`; the post-click counter only catches DOM `submit` events.
* **New:** a page that never goes quiet for legitimate reasons (clock, live-chat widget, polling) is refused with `page_not_settled`; timeout and quiet window are policy knobs. Settling does not observe iframes, web sockets or CSS-only transitions.
* **New:** the page-side fetch/XHR counters are installed once and lost on a full navigation (then reported as `document_navigation`).
* Values lost on an *unmounted* step cannot be re-checked without going Back (noted, not detected). No Back/revisit navigation, no multi-page server flows, no file upload, no drag-based widgets.
* The fixtures are authored by us; the stage ground truth encodes our assumption of when a control "should" appear. 26 small fixtures say nothing about real-portal reliability.
* A walk that stops early or unconfirmed reports `NEEDS_REVIEW`, not `FAILED`, unless a filled answer failed verification. A fully successful run therefore always needs a person (or a future final-step policy) to confirm completion and submit.
* Image inputs outside a form are treated like type-less buttons outside a form (they cannot submit); an `<input type=image>` handled by script that posts via `fetch` is not detectable from markup.
